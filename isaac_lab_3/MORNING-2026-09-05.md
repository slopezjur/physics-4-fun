# Morning report — overnight sim-to-sim session, 2026-09-04 → 05

## The headline

**Two real, independent geometry defects were found and fixed — one on each side of the boundary.**
Both had been silently wrong for the entire history of this project.

1. **Godot (Jolt) enforced every asymmetric joint limit MIRRORED** — `physical = [-upper, -lower]`.
   The dummy could not bend its knees in the direction a gait needs; its hip mainly extended
   backwards. Commanding the knee to -2.0 rad parked it at -0.100005.
2. **Every BOX collider in the Isaac USD was exactly HALF its authored size**, including the FEET
   (0.11 × 0.06 × 0.04 instead of 0.22 × 0.12 × 0.08). Isaac trained every policy this project has
   produced on a dummy with half Godot's fore-aft support base.

Neither was findable by reading config: the limits matched their declaration in both files, and the
capsule limbs and total mass (80.6 kg) matched exactly. Only measuring the delivered behaviour found
them.

## What did NOT work, measured

Flight penalties (twice, -5.0 and -20.0 — the policy bought speed and paid the tax). Compliance
modelling in Isaac (both frames, worse than leaving it rigid). 240 Hz physics (adopted, then
REVERSED — it halves static sag and makes the dynamics worse). Jolt solver tuning (redistributes
chatter, never removes it). Symmetry-breaking at spawn. Pushes. Faster commands. Widened
randomisation. Eight retrain fragments on the corrected body.

## LATE RESULT: Isaac now genuinely walks — and Godot still takes zero steps

Two reward defects were found in the last two hours, both of the same kind: a term that described
the SHAPE of walking without requiring the walking.

* `feet_air_time` paid per touchdown for airtime, and a two-footed HOP collects it on both feet
  every cycle. Removing it halved the flight phase.
* `rew_single_support` paid for "exactly one foot down" gated on a command being PRESENT, so the
  policy stood on ONE LEG - 93.8% single support, 36 steps... no: 2 steps, and **vx = 0.00 m/s**.
  Fixed by multiplying by `drive` (achieved-over-commanded speed) so it only pays as part of
  progress.

After that fix, Isaac walks properly:

    ISAAC   double 5.7%   SINGLE 94.3%   flight 0.0%   36 foot strikes   vx +0.59 m/s

**The same checkpoint takes ZERO foot strikes in Godot at authority 0.10, 0.15 and 0.20.**

## THE MECHANISM (this is the night's main result)

**The same policy walks in Isaac and HOPS in Godot.** Authority 0.10, Godot's upright window:

| | double | SINGLE | flight | feet in same state |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Godot** | 72.8% | **5.5%** | 21.7% | **94.5%** |
| **Isaac** | 5.7% | **94.3%** | 0.0% | - |

`corr(footZ_L, footZ_R) = +0.942` — both Godot feet rise and fall together. The cause is a
whole-body vertical mode:

| | pelvis vertical range | bounce |
|---|---|---|
| **Godot 120 Hz** | **0.227 m** | 1.04 Hz |
| Isaac | 0.075 m | 1.60 Hz |

**Godot's compliant legs plus an 80 kg body are a spring-mass oscillator at about 1 Hz.** Isaac's
joints are rigid and have no such mode. The gait excites Godot's bounce, the bounce lifts both feet
at once, and the walk becomes a hop — the same shape the POLICY had before the reward fix, now
produced by the BODY.

**Stiffening kills it, confirming the diagnosis:**

| Godot rate | pelvis range | single support | flight | upright (15 s run) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 120 Hz | 0.227 m | 5.5% | 21.7% | 290/840 |
| **240 Hz** | **0.002 m** | 0.0% | 0.0% | **840/840** |

227 mm of bounce to 2 mm. `project.godot` is now at 240 Hz for this reason — and yes, I reverted it
to 120 earlier in the night and then reinstated it. The revert was based on open-loop replay, which
was measured while both policies hopped; at 120 Hz the pogo happens to follow a hopping action
sequence better. Right number, wrong regime.

**And at 240 Hz it still does not walk — it stands perfectly still.** Removing the bounce reveals
the policy's true commanded behaviour in Godot: a static posture, 0% single support, no
displacement. So the last problem is the observation fixed point, not the body.

## Superseded: the lift-deficit framing

Driving Godot with a SCRIPTED gait at maximum command takes the policy out of the question:

| | hip max | knee max | foot strikes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Isaac, walking | 0.515 | 0.570 | **36** |
| Godot, max scripted command @0.15 | 0.385 | **0.535** | **0** |
| Godot, max scripted command @0.30 | 0.728 | 1.320 | 2 |

**Godot swings its knee as far as Isaac does and the foot never leaves the ground.** That rules out,
by measurement, the entire actuator side - gains, effort ceilings, the Hill law, action scale,
command amplitude. The leg moves. The contact does not break. Godot needs roughly twice the joint
excursion before a foot lifts at all.

**Start here in the morning:** instrument FOOT HEIGHT and per-foot contact force in Godot. The trace
currently carries only the height-derived contact flag and joint angles, so there is no measurement
of whether the swing foot is rising and being held, or not rising at all. Every remaining hypothesis
- weight transfer, stance-leg compression, contact resolution - needs that number, and it does not
exist yet.

## Earlier status (superseded above)

The best checkpoints stand at authority 0.10-0.15 and take 1-4 steps in 20 s. Upright displacement -
distance covered while still standing, a metric added tonight after a checkpoint scored +1.01 m by
falling over and sliding - stays under 0.15 m.

**The mechanism is understood.** In Godot the policy parks at a constant posture with both feet
planted; in Isaac the same checkpoint oscillates its hips and knees around zero. The policy is
memoryless, so its gait phase lives entirely in the observation: Godot's feet never lift, contacts
stay (1,1), the swing-phase signal never arrives, and the posture holds. A fixed point of the closed
loop. The asymmetric action mapping deepens it - a positive knee action maps to span 0.1 and moves
the joint 0.013 rad, so the policy is parked in the dead half of its own action space.

## THE LAST AND CLEANEST RESULT: Isaac overshoots its joint targets, Godot tracks them

Built `scripts/replay_isaac.py` — Isaac's mirror of Godot's `ReplayActionsPath` — so for the first
time the SAME action sequence drives both engines with no policy in either loop. Every earlier
"Godot vs Isaac" comparison differed by both the body AND the policy's response to it.

Commanded hip target is 0.315 rad, knee -0.390:

| | hip reached | knee reached | foot contact |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Godot** | **+0.382** (1.2x target) | **-0.471** (1.2x) | **100%** |
| **Isaac** | **+0.833** (2.6x target) | **-1.054** (2.7x) | 65% |

**Isaac's joints fly past their commanded angles by more than twice; Godot's track them.** Isaac's
feet lift because of that overshoot. Godot's never leave the ground.

This inverts the assumption behind most of the night's work. The sag measurements are real — Godot
is compliant under STATIC load — but in the dynamic regime that matters for walking the deficit runs
the other way: **Godot is the well-behaved tracker and Isaac is wildly under-damped.** Every attempt
to close the gap by stiffening Godot was pushing the wrong side.

Neither obvious remedy works: target-space damping (2.6x -> 2.0x, and worse at higher values) nor
more XPBD iterations (16 helps slightly, 32 is worse). Both non-monotonic. The overshoot is momentum
the position projection does not absorb.

## Where I would go next

1. **Start from the overshoot, not the sag.** The one-number statement of the gap is that Isaac
   reaches 2.6x its commanded joint angle and Godot reaches 1.2x. Everything else measured tonight

2. **Re-take every plant measurement.** Most of `SIM-TO-SIM.md` was measured with half-size box
